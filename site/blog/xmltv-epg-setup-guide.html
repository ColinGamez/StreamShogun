XMLTV EPG Setup Guide: From Empty Guide to Full Schedule

A playlist without guide data is just a wall of channel names. XMLTV is the format that adds "what's on"—this guide explains the format, how channels get matched, and how to build one reliable guide from several sources.

EPG—electronic programme guide—is the schedule layer: titles, start times, descriptions, and episode information for each channel. The de facto standard format for exchanging it is XMLTV, an XML dialect that has been around since the early 2000s and is understood by nearly every serious player and guide tool.

The good news: the format is simple and you can read it in a text editor. The bad news: a playlist and a guide only connect when their channel identifiers agree, and that agreement is where most setups break down.

What an XMLTV file actually contains

An XMLTV document has two kinds of records. Channels declare who the guide knows about, and programmes attach schedule entries to those channel IDs:

<tv>
  <channel id="BBCOne.uk">
    <display-name>BBC One</display-name>
    <icon src="https://example.com/bbc1.png"/>
  </channel>
  <programme channel="BBCOne.uk" start="20261104200000 +0000"
             stop="20261104210000 +0000">
    <title>Evening News</title>
    <desc>The day's top stories.</desc>
    <category>News</category>
    <episode-num system="xmltv_ns">0.4.0/1</episode-num>
  </programme>
</tv>

Details worth knowing when troubleshooting:

  • Timestamps use YYYYMMDDhhmmss plus a UTC offset like +0900. A correct parser converts everything to UTC—if your guide appears shifted by whole hours, the source's offsets are wrong or missing.
  • Channel IDs are arbitrary strings chosen by the guide publisher. There is no global registry, which is exactly why matching to your playlist matters.
  • Files are often gzipped (a .xml.gz URL) because a week of schedules for hundreds of channels compresses extremely well. A good player detects and decompresses gzip automatically.

Where guide data comes from

XMLTV files are produced by grabbers—tools that collect schedules from broadcasters or listings services—or published directly by guide providers. Common legitimate origins include a broadcaster's own published feed, a listings service your subscription entitles you to, community-maintained open guide projects for public channels, and grabbers you run yourself against sources you may access.

Whatever the source, treat a guide like any other data feed: prefer publishers that document their coverage and update cadence, check that the channels you actually watch are included, and confirm the schedule timezone behavior before trusting it for recordings or reminders.

How playlist channels connect to guide channels

The bridge between your M3U playlist and an XMLTV guide is one attribute in the playlist: tvg-id. When a playlist entry's tvg-id equals a channel id in the guide, matching is exact and immediate.

Real playlists rarely cooperate. IDs are missing, or the playlist publisher and the guide publisher chose different ID schemes. Then matching falls back to names—and NEWS 24 | HD does not equal News 24 until something normalizes case, punctuation, quality tokens, and country prefixes. That normalization is what Smart Channel Matching does, with a confidence score so you can review the suggestions that are merely plausible rather than obvious.

If your guide is empty after setup, this matching step is almost always the reason: the guide loaded fine, but no playlist channel could be confidently linked to a guide channel.

  1. STEP 01

    In StreamShōgun, open your EPG sources and add the guide URL you are authorized to use—plain .xml and gzipped .xml.gz endpoints both work. Name it after its coverage, e.g. "UK free-to-air — weekly".

  2. STEP 02

    After the first fetch, confirm the guide actually contains the channels you watch. Coverage problems are source problems—no amount of local tweaking fixes a guide that simply doesn't list your channels.

  3. STEP 03

    Run channel matching

    Open the matching view. Exact tvg-id hits attach automatically; name-based suggestions arrive with confidence scores. Approve the obvious ones in bulk and reserve your attention for the low-confidence tail.

  4. STEP 04

    Fix the stragglers manually

    For channels that refuse to match, assign the guide entry by hand. Manual assignments stick, so this is a one-time tax per channel rather than a recurring chore.

  5. STEP 05

    Enable auto-refresh

    Schedules go stale—typically within days. With Pro, auto-refresh re-pulls your guide sources on a schedule and re-runs matching, so the guide stays current without manual fetches.

Merging multiple EPG sources

No single guide covers everything. A realistic setup combines a regional guide with one or two channel-specific feeds. Multi-EPG merge handles this: each source contributes its channels, and programmes for the same matched channel combine into one timeline instead of competing duplicates.

Two habits keep a merged guide trustworthy:

  • Prefer one source per channel. When two guides list the same channel with conflicting schedules, decide which publisher you trust rather than letting the overlap surprise you mid-evening.
  • Watch the refresh cadence. A source that updates weekly will show empty cells for the days beyond its horizon. Knowing each source's update rhythm explains gaps before you debug them.

Frequently asked questions

Is XMLTV the only EPG format?

It is the most widely supported interchange format, and the one StreamShōgun consumes. Some providers offer proprietary JSON APIs, but XMLTV's age means virtually every guide tool can produce or read it.

Why does my playlist have tvg-id values that match nothing?

The playlist publisher wrote IDs for a guide scheme you are not using. IDs are only meaningful when playlist and guide share a scheme—otherwise fall back to normalized name matching or manual assignment.

How big can an XMLTV file get?

Large global guides reach tens of megabytes compressed and far more uncompressed. StreamShōgun validates and decompresses with strict size limits so a malformed or hostile feed cannot exhaust your machine.

Does StreamShōgun provide guide data?

No. It fetches, merges, and matches XMLTV sources you connect. No channels, schedules, or streams are bundled with the app.
